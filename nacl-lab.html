Why Can't You See the Salt?

Every Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ion is still there. Each one is now completely surrounded by water molecules — a hydration shell. The ions are spread apart and wrapped in water so thoroughly that they're invisible to your eye. Not gone. Hidden by water. That's what "dissolved" means.

Phase 2 — Observe

Run It. Watch Carefully.

Switch to the Simulation tab, press "Drop Salt" and answer while watching.

Edge ions have fewer neighbors holding them. They're bonded on 2-3 sides instead of 4. Water attacks the exposed sides first. Dissolution always starts at the surface and works inward.
Oxygen (red, δ⁻) faces Na⁺ — negative attracts positive. Hydrogen (white, δ⁺) faces Cl⁻ — positive attracts negative. This is the ion-dipole interaction that makes dissolving possible.
A hydration shell forms — 5-6 water molecules leave the surrounding water and arrange themselves around the freed ion, oriented with their charged ends facing inward. The blue glow represents this shell. It stabilizes the ion and prevents it from recombining with the crystal.
Phase 3 — Explain

Make Sense of What You Saw

Use vocabulary from the Vocabulary tab.

NaCl exists as a crystal lattice where Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions are held by ionic bonds. Water molecules are polar — oxygen is δ⁻, hydrogen is δ⁺. They orient so oxygen faces Na⁺ and hydrogen faces Cl⁻. These ion-dipole forces pull edge ions until they overcome the ionic bonds. Once freed, 5-6 water molecules surround each ion forming a hydration shell that stabilizes it. The salt "disappears" because each ion is individually caged in water — not gone, just hidden.
Incorrect. The Free Na⁺ and Free Cl⁻ counters prove every ion still exists. They were separated and hydrated, not destroyed. If you evaporated the water, the ions would find each other and reform the crystal. Dissolved ≠ destroyed.
Water is polar — it has partial charges that create ion-dipole forces strong enough to pull ions from the lattice. Oil is nonpolar — no partial charges, no ion-dipole interaction, no dissolving. Like dissolves like.
Phase 4 — Extend

Push Beyond the Simulation

These require thinking, not searching.

Sugar has -OH groups on its surface that are polar. Water forms hydrogen bonds with these groups, pulling sugar molecules apart individually. No ions form — the molecule stays intact. That's why sugar water doesn't conduct electricity.
NaCl produces free Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions — charged particles that carry current. Sugar dissolves as whole neutral molecules with no charge. No free charges = no conductivity. NaCl is an electrolyte; sugar is a non-electrolyte.
